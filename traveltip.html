Practical Advice

8 Essential Travel Tips

Hard-won advice from frequent India travellers — read it before you pack.

01

Packing Essentials

India rewards light, breathable packing. Layer for AC trains and hill stations, and remember that laundry is cheap and everywhere. Leave room — you'll want to buy textiles, spices and handicrafts.

Quick tips
  • Light cottons + one warm layer for AC and hills
  • Modest scarf for temples (covers shoulders/head)
  • High-SPF sunscreen, hat, sunglasses
  • Power bank & universal adapter (Type C/D/M)
  • Comfortable slip-off shoes (you'll remove them often)
02

Budget Travel

India is one of the world's most rewarding budget destinations. The same daily spend can buy a dorm bed or a heritage-suite splurge — it all depends on how you mix trains, homestays and street food.

Quick tips
  • Book trains on IRCTC 60+ days ahead; Tatkal opens at 10 a.m.
  • Night sleeper trains save a hotel night
  • Eat where locals queue — high turnover = fresh food
  • SIM card: Airtel/Jio at any airport counter
  • Bargain in markets, never in fixed-price shops
03

Health & Safety

India is overwhelmingly safe for prepared travellers. The two things that catch most people out are Delhi-belly and dehydration — both easy to prevent with a few habits.

Quick tips
  • Drink only sealed bottled or filtered water — no ice
  • Carry ORS sachets; rehydrate before you feel thirsty
  • Keep digital + paper copies of passport & visa
  • Use hotel safes; carry a small padlock for trains
  • Emergency numbers: Police 100, Ambulance 102
04

Cultural Etiquette

India's social codes are layered but warm — a little effort goes a long way. The head wobble means "yes, okay, I understand", and "namaste" with pressed palms works everywhere.

Quick tips
  • Remove shoes before temples, mosques and homes
  • Dress modestly at sacred sites — cover knees & shoulders
  • Use your right hand for eating, giving and receiving
  • Ask before photographing people or rituals
  • Don't point with your feet or touch someone's head
05

Getting Around

India's transport is vast, varied and an experience in itself. The trick is matching the mode to the distance — metros for cities, trains for regions, flights for cross-country jumps.

Quick tips
  • Metro rail in Delhi/Mumbai/Bengaluru/Hyderabad/Chennai
  • Auto-rickshaws: insist on the meter or fix the fare first
  • Ola & Uber work reliably in major cities
  • Overnight Volvo buses beat mountain driving
  • Domestic flights (IndiGo) are cheap when booked early
06

Food & Water

Indian street food is some of the best on earth — and safe when you follow a few simple rules. "Less spicy" (kam mirchi) is a phrase worth knowing; raita and lassi are natural coolers.

Quick tips
  • Choose busy stalls — high turnover means fresh food
  • Fried > raw: avoid pre-cut fruit from unknown vendors
  • Vegetarians: look for "pure veg" restaurants (green sign)
  • Carry a SteriPen or LifeStraw for remote areas
  • Try thalis — a complete regional meal on one tray
07

Solo Travel

Solo travel in India is rewarding and increasingly common. Solo women travellers should research area-specific advice and prefer homestays and women-only dorms where available.

Quick tips
  • Safe picks: Kerala, Himachal, Pondicherry, Rishikesh
  • Share your live location with someone back home
  • Join day-tour groups to meet other travellers
  • Avoid unlit lanes after dark in smaller towns
  • Trust your instincts — say no firmly to touts
08

Photography Tips

India is a photographer's dream — but a few courtesies matter. Golden hour at the ghats, blue hour at the forts, and the chaos of a bazaar all reward patience over speed.

Quick tips
  • Always ask before photographing people or rituals
  • Some temples ban cameras inside the sanctum
  • Golden hour (6–8 a.m., 5–6 p.m.) is magical
  • Carry extra SD cards — you'll shoot more than expected
  • Drone use requires a DGCA permit — check first

Did You Know?

India spans 29 states, 7 union territories and 22 official languages — yet you can travel almost everywhere with English alone. The rupee symbol ₹ was adopted in 2010, and ₹100 buys a hearty street meal in most cities.
